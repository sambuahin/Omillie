Biography of the Late MRS. Millicent Buahin

Read by: Mr. Eric Kwaku Bempong

Mrs. Millicent Ameley Buahin (née Lartey), also known as Omillie, was born on December 19, 1940, at Gbese, a suburb of Accra, to the late Robert Lartey and Mary Adei Lartey, both of blessed memory.

As the firstborn of the family, she embraced the responsibility of helping her parents raise her younger siblings. This experience strengthened and prepared her to become a devoted mother, grandmother, and great‑grandmother.

Mrs. Millicent Buahin began her early education at Accra Royal School. After completing her elementary education, she worked as a trader. She was later employed by the National Lotteries Authority, where she served as a Lotto Receiver.

In her personal life, Mrs. Millicent Ameley Buahin was married to Mr. Israel Kwabena Buahin. They were blessed with three children: Mr. Gordon Buahin, Miss Providence Buahin, and Miss Judith Buahin. Prior to her marriage to Mr. Buahin, she had four older children: Mr. Clifford Nii Teiko Turkson, Mrs. Henrietta Marian Dennis, Mr. Henry Obeng Ampah, and Mr. Samuel Apersi Ampah. She also raised many of her cousins, nieces, and nephews in her household. In addition, her home served as a refuge for some of her siblings whenever they needed support.

Mrs. Millicent Ameley Buahin was one of the founding members of the Ebenezer Methodist Church, which began at Mr. King’s residence near the Dansoman Roundabout.

Due to her diligence and love for the work of God, she was appointed a Bible Class Leader. Mrs. Millicent Buahin once served as secretary of the Women’s Fellowship of the Ebenezer Society. Her hard work was recognized by her fellow sisters, who later elected her as president of the Women’s Fellowship. She also served as a onetime caretaker of the Ebenezer Methodist Girls’ Fellowship. Her love for music inspired her to join the Singing Band and the Choir, where she was eventually appointed as the alto part leader.

She cherished relationships and always ensured that everyone around her felt comfortable and loved. Her hospitality extended even to strangers, and she gave cheerfully to all who crossed her path. Mrs. Millicent Ameley Buahin was a very lively person, and her vibrant spirit made everyone who knew her feel excited in her presence. Her presence was always felt at every social gathering she attended.

On 23rd November 2025, Mrs. Millicent Ameley Buahin was called by her Maker to eternal glory. The family has lost a mighty oak tree, but we take solace in the Lord, knowing that although she is no longer with us, we shall meet her again someday.

Her light‑hearted spirit, delicious meals, and playful jokes at family gatherings will be fondly remembered.

On this day, as we bid her farewell, we say ayekoo to Omillie for a good work done.

Rest in Peace, till we meet again.

Fare thee well, Ameley.

Due, due, Yaawɔ Ojogbann!
